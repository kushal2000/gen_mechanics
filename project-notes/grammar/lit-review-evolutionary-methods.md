# Literature review: evolutionary-computation theory for a balanced grammar and mutation operators

Scope: EC theory relevant to designing a generative grammar (tree of palm bodies and digits) and its
mutation operators for evolving robot-hand kinematics against one shared learned controller, under
noisy fitness and a small generation budget (our current run: ~1,024 designs, 40 generations, top-50%
truncation, one offspring per survivor — treated below as one instance of a budget, not the definition
of it). Compiled via WebSearch/WebFetch across three independent, cross-checking research passes; no
repository cloning or large downloads. Every claim is tagged **[verified]** (a primary source — PDF,
arXiv page, or publisher abstract page carrying the actual finding — was opened and read this session)
or **[snippet]** (search-result summary, secondary source, or well-established domain knowledge not
re-opened this session). No citation, DOI, or URL below is fabricated; where a source could not be
confirmed this is stated explicitly rather than guessed at.

## 1. Genotype-phenotype mapping and locality

| Work | Year | Key result | Relevance to our grammar | URL/DOI | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rothlauf, *Representations for Genetic and Evolutionary Algorithms* | 2002/2006 (2nd ed.) | Defines a representation's quality via three properties — **locality** (do neighboring genotypes map to neighboring phenotypes), **redundancy** (ratio of genotype-space to phenotype-space size, synonymous vs. non-synonymous), and **scaling** (how genotype size grows with problem size) — and argues locality is the dominant determinant of GA/EA search performance: low-locality representations turn small mutations into large, uncorrelated phenotype jumps and degrade hill-climbing regardless of operator tuning. | Directly frames what our "small-step operators are local, structural ones moderately local" measurements are measuring, and gives the vocabulary (locality/redundancy/scaling) used throughout this review. | Springer, ISBN 978-3-540-25059-3; restated in https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3638530.3648426 | [snippet] |
| Sendhoff, Kreutz, von Seelen, "A Condition for the Genotype-Phenotype Mapping: Causality" | 1997 | Formalizes **strong causality**: small genotypic changes must, under a *given* mutation operator, produce small phenotype/fitness changes with high probability (a symmetric conditional-probability requirement); **weak causality** is the degenerate case where genotype- and phenotype-neighborhood relations are statistically independent. Proposes measuring causality empirically as these conditional probabilities over sampled mutation events, and derives constraints this places on structural mutation operators (e.g., for evolving network topologies). | Gives a measurable, testable, *per-operator* definition of locality tied to the mutation operator itself, not just the encoding — exactly the object our E1 locality experiment probed when calling different operators "local" vs "moderately local." | https://arxiv.org/abs/adap-org/9711001 | [verified] |
| Rothlauf & Oetzel, "On the Locality of Grammatical Evolution" | 2006 (EuroGP, LNCS 3905:320-330) | Shows Grammatical Evolution's genotype (integer/binary codon string) → phenotype (derivation tree) mapping has **poor locality**: a single-codon point mutation early in the genome can change which production is selected at that point, which — because subsequent codons are re-read and reinterpreted against the new derivation context — cascades into large, uncorrelated changes throughout the rest of the tree (a ripple/avalanche effect); mutation-based local search performs worse under GE's linear-plus-modulo mapping than under a representation that mutates the derivation tree directly. | The closest published analogue to a "ripple effect from an indirect linear encoding" pathology; validates our design choice to mutate the derivation tree directly (Whigham-style) rather than through a GE-style linear genome, and to measure locality operator-by-operator rather than assume one grammar-wide score. | https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/11729976_29 ; findings page https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/eurogp06_RothlaufOetzel.html | [verified] (abstract/findings page and cached extracts opened; full paywalled text not read) |
| Raidl & Gottlieb, "Empirical Analysis of Locality, Heritability and Heuristic Bias in Evolutionary Algorithms: A Case Study for the Multidimensional Knapsack Problem" | 2005 | Compares six representations (direct and decoder/indirect) empirically, operationalizing locality as the correlation between genotype distance (edit/Hamming) and phenotype distance after decoding, and heritability as the correlation between offspring fitness and parent fitness; finds representation choice dominates operator choice for search performance on the benchmark problem, and that locality should be measured per (representation, operator) pair, not assumed from inspecting the encoding. | Gives a concrete, reusable recipe: report a genotype-distance-vs-phenotype-distance (or Δfitness) correlation per operator class, replacing our qualitative "local / moderately local" labels with a number; reinforces that locality is a property of (grammar, operator) pairs, matching our finding that different structural operators on the same grammar have different locality. | *Evolutionary Computation* 13(4):441-475, MIT Press. https://direct.mit.edu/evco/article-abstract/13/4/441/1221 | [snippet] |
| Jones & Forrest, fitness-distance correlation (FDC) | 1995 | Establishes fitness-distance correlation — correlating a distance metric (genotype-space or search-step distance) against fitness difference across sampled pairs — as the standard way to operationalize whether a landscape is exploitable by local/hill-climbing search; strongly negative correlation indicates an exploitable landscape. | The general-purpose statistic underlying both the Rothlauf and Raidl & Gottlieb locality measures above; could replace our qualitative operator-locality labels with a single correlation coefficient per operator. | Widely cited (ICGA 1995); not independently opened this session. | [snippet] |

**How locality is measured, synthesized:** three converging, mutually consistent operational recipes appear in this literature — (a) Sendhoff et al.'s strong-causality conditional probability test, (b) fitness/genotype-distance correlation (Jones & Forrest; Raidl & Gottlieb), and (c) Rothlauf's "mean phenotype distance conditioned on fixed genotype distance." All three are *empirical and per-operator*, not global grammar properties, which matches how our own experiments already report locality per operator class rather than for the grammar as a whole.

## 2. Grammar-based evolution

| Work | Year | Key result | Relevance to our grammar | URL/DOI | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| O'Neill & Ryan, "Grammatical Evolution: Evolving Programs in an Arbitrary Language" | 2001 | Introduces Grammatical Evolution: a linear genome of integer "codons" is mapped onto a BNF grammar by, at each nonterminal expansion, choosing production `codon mod (#alternatives)`; if the genome is exhausted before the derivation completes, reading wraps back to codon 0 up to a wrap limit, after which incomplete individuals are invalidated. | The canonical example of a grammar-derived representation whose *encoding indirection* (not the grammar itself) causes the ripple-effect locality failure in §1; our grammar instead mutates the derivation tree directly, sidestepping this specific failure mode by construction. | *IEEE Trans. Evolutionary Computation* 5(4):349-358. https://www.brinckerhoff.org/tmp/grammatica_evolution_ieee_tec_2001.pdf | [verified] |
| Whigham, "Grammatically-based Genetic Programming" | 1995 | Introduces grammar-guided GP that operates directly on the derivation tree induced by a context-free grammar, with crossover/mutation constrained to swap subtrees rooted at nonterminals of matching type — giving the grammar explicit control over syntactic/typing bias without GE's linear-genome indirection. | Architecturally what our grammar already does (typed tree of bodies/digits/phalanges, structural operators constrained by production type); direct precedent and justification for derivation-tree mutation over a GE-style linear mapping. | https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/whigham_1995_GBGP.html | [snippet] |
| Koza, Automatically Defined Functions (ADFs) | 1994 (*Genetic Programming II*) | Adds separately-evolved, reusable subtree "functions" callable from the main program, giving evolution an explicit mechanism to discover and reuse modular substructure instead of re-deriving it independently each time it is needed; improves solution compactness/quality on problems with regular substructure. | Analogy for whether repeated digit/phalanx motifs should become explicitly reusable, parameterized productions (a "digit template") rather than independently regrown structures each time — a design lever for the production set, not just the operators. | MIT Press, 1994 (book; not independently re-verified this session). | [snippet] |
| Nguyen Xuan Hoai et al., Tree-Adjoining Grammar-guided GP (TAG3P) | 2001-2003 | Replaces context-free substitution-only derivation with tree-adjoining grammars, whose **adjunction** operator inserts an auxiliary tree at an internal node without altering the rest of the derivation — a strictly more local structural edit than CFG-GP's subtree-swap crossover/mutation, and one that lets the grammar directly encode recursive/repeating motifs. | Directly analogous to what a well-behaved "insert a phalanx" operator should look like: TAG's adjunction *is* a formalized, local structural-insertion operator, worth comparing against our ad hoc insertion prior/operator. | "A Framework for Tree Adjunct Grammar Guided Genetic Programming," PACCS 2001; https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/3-540-45984-7_22 | [snippet] |
| Hornby & Pollack, "Body-Brain Co-evolution Using L-systems as a Generative Encoding" | 2001 (GECCO) | An L-system (parametric rewrite grammar) generates both morphology and controller from one shared symbol alphabet; because the same production rule is reused at multiple derivation points, the generative (rule-based) encoding produced creatures with an order of magnitude more parts and markedly higher **regularity** (related, repeated structure) than an equivalent direct/non-generative encoding evolved under the same budget. | Direct precedent for our grammar being generative (productions reused across digits/phalanges) rather than a flat parameter vector; their central empirical claim — generativity/regularity is what buys scalability to more parts under a fixed budget — is exactly the design bet our grammar makes. | GECCO 2001, pp. 868-875; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/2359681 | [snippet] |
| Attribute grammars in GP | general | Attaches synthesized/inherited attributes (type, arity, remaining "budget") to grammar symbols so productions can be constrained by derivation context (e.g., "no more than N phalanges left in this branch's budget"), giving semantic well-formedness constraints beyond bare context-free syntax. | Relevant if production probabilities or eligibility should depend on remaining motor/structural budget (ties to our cost term) rather than being static per nonterminal. | General GP literature; no single canonical primary source opened this session. | [snippet] |
| "Initialisation and Grammar Design in Grammar-Guided Evolutionary Computation" | 2022 (arXiv) | States that **unbalanced production sets** — nonterminals whose alternative productions differ greatly in expected size contribution, or grammars where recursive productions vastly outnumber terminating ones for some nonterminal — create a structural bias toward particular derivation-tree sizes/depths *independent of selection*, purely as an artifact of grammar shape; recommends checking the branching factor and expected-size contribution of each production alternative, validated empirically via size histograms under uniform random derivation, before running evolution. | The most directly relevant published statement of what "a balanced production set" means: balance is a property of the *production-alternative size distribution per nonterminal*, checkable exactly the way we checked it ("neutral drift relaxes toward the growth prior" is diagnostic of an unchecked instance of this). | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.07410 | [verified] |

**What "balanced" production sets mean, synthesized:** two distinct senses recur across GE/GGGP/TAG work and should be kept separate: (i) **grammar (prior) size-neutrality** — the production alternatives for a nonterminal, and the grammar as a whole, should have no inherent expected-growth bias under *uniform random derivation itself* (Ryan/Azad-style "sensible initialisation" targets exactly this); (ii) **operator (prior) size-neutrality** — the mutation/insertion operators sampled from the grammar should not carry a size/shape bias different from the grammar's own base distribution, otherwise mutation-only drift will relax the population toward the *operator's* implicit prior rather than staying near the *grammar's* prior — precisely our observed "neutral drift relaxes toward the growth prior."

## 3. Bloat and parsimony

| Work | Year | Key result | Relevance to our grammar | URL/DOI | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Langdon, Soule, Poli, Foster, "Fitness Causes Bloat" | 1997/1998 | Comparing runs with and without fitness-based selection on the same variable-length representation shows program/tree-size growth is driven by *selection* acting on a fixed-size fitness landscape embedded in a variable-length search space: above a certain size there are increasingly many near-neutral longer variants, so selection's random walk among near-equal-fitness variants is asymmetric and biased toward growth even with no explicit size reward. | The classical "bloat is a selection artifact" result; contrasts with our mutation-only neutral-drift finding, implying our grammar can bloat/shrink via two *independent* mechanisms (selection-driven and operator-prior-driven) that need separate diagnostics. | Univ. Birmingham TR CSRP-97-09; EuroGP'98 version http://www0.cs.ucl.ac.uk/staff/W.Langdon/ftp/papers/WBL.euro98_bloatm.pdf | [snippet] |
| Soule & Foster, "Removal Bias: a New Cause of Code Growth in Tree-Based Evolutionary Programming" | 1998 | Identifies **removal bias**: under subtree crossover, inactive/non-contributing (intron-like) code sits disproportionately in smaller-than-average subtrees near the leaves, so excising-and-replacing such a subtree tends to produce offspring that are bigger but no less fit — a fitness-neutral, size-increasing move invisible to selection that accumulates over generations. | A crossover-specific mechanism (inapplicable to our asexual, one-offspring-per-survivor scheme as stated), but the *general principle* — an asymmetric size effect that is fitness-neutral gets retained and compounds — is directly analogous to our neutral-drift-toward-growth-prior finding via a different operator mechanism (intrinsic operator size prior rather than subtree-size asymmetry). | IEEE ICEC 1998; reviewed in Silva & Costa, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10710-008-9075-9 | [snippet] |
| Poli & McPhee, "size evolution equation" | ~2001-2008 | Derives an exact recursion for mean program size across one generation from schema theory, connected to Price's theorem: mean size next generation depends on the covariance between program size and selection probability (fitness) across size classes. Under purely neutral/symmetric operators with **no selection**, the equation reduces to a flat, unbiased random walk in size — growth or shrinkage requires either selection correlated with size, or an asymmetric operator. | Gives the exact theoretical decomposition for our neutral-drift result: under mutation-only, no-selection conditions the equation predicts *zero expected drift*; any directional drift we observe must come from the operator's own size-change distribution being asymmetric (e.g., "add" and "remove" not applied as matched-probability, matched-magnitude inverses), not from anything the equation itself calls bloat. This makes the fix operator symmetry, not selection. | https://cswww.essex.ac.uk/staff/rpoli/gp-field-guide/113Bloat.html (fetch failed — DNS; cited via converging secondary description) | [snippet] |
| Poli, "A Simple but Theoretically-Motivated Method to Control Bloat in Genetic Programming" (**Tarpeian method**) | 2003 (EuroGP, LNCS 2610) | For each offspring above a size threshold, assigns a fitness of zero *without evaluating it*, with fixed probability p (not 1) — a stochastic pre-evaluation "fitness hole" derived from the size evolution equation above to drive the size-fitness covariance toward zero; cheap because it skips expensive evaluation for the killed fraction, and tunable via p. | Directly reusable here: because fitness evaluation (controller training/rollout) is the expensive, noisy step, a Tarpeian-style *pre-evaluation* size gate bounds structural growth without ever touching the noisy fitness signal and without spending controller-training budget on oversized offspring. | https://repository.essex.ac.uk/14600/ | [snippet] |
| Poli & McPhee, "Covariant Parsimony Pressure for Genetic Programming" | 2008 | Sets the linear parsimony coefficient to c ≈ Cov(size, fitness)/Var(size), estimated fresh each generation, so as to exactly cancel the selection-driven size-fitness covariance and hold mean size stationary (or on a chosen schedule) instead of using a hand-tuned constant. | An alternative to Tarpeian acting on the fitness signal itself; less attractive under noisy fitness since it requires a *reliable* per-generation size-fitness covariance estimate, which is exactly the statistic noise corrupts. | GECCO 2008, pp. 1267-1274; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228347495 | [snippet] |
| Luke & Panait, lexicographic parsimony pressure & double tournament | 2002 | **Lexicographic parsimony**: when two individuals are fitness-tied (or statistically indistinguishable), prefer the smaller as a tie-break, otherwise fitness dominates entirely. **Double tournament**: a preliminary tournament on size feeds a subsequent tournament on fitness (or vice versa), with a parameter controlling how strongly size vs. fitness dominates, avoiding any need to combine size and fitness into one hand-tuned numeric penalty. | Lexicographic parsimony only engages its size preference precisely when fitness differences are within noise — exactly when a well-defined secondary criterion should dominate anyway — making it the most noise-robust of the parsimony mechanisms surveyed, cheaper and less fragile than covariance-based methods under our noisy fitness. | GECCO 2002; https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/645826.669585 | [snippet] |
| Langdon, "Size Fair and Homologous Tree Genetic Programming Crossovers" | 2000 | Standard subtree crossover increases mean tree size roughly linearly per generation; size-fair crossover biases the second parent's crossover-point choice toward subtrees whose size matches the excised subtree, and homologous crossover further aligns crossover points by structural position — both substantially reduce bloat without loss of GP performance. | Not directly applicable (no crossover), but the design principle transfers exactly: make a structural operator's size-change distribution explicitly symmetric/matched by construction rather than accepting whatever an ad hoc implementation induces — the concrete fix implied by the size evolution equation for our add/remove structural operators. | *Genetic Programming and Evolvable Machines* 1:95-119; http://www0.cs.ucl.ac.uk/staff/W.Langdon/ftp/papers/WBL_fairxo.pdf | [snippet] |
| Silva & Costa, dynamic/adaptive depth and size limits | ~2009 | Instead of a fixed a-priori depth/size ceiling, adapts the limit each generation (e.g., to just above the current best individual's size), tightening as the population's useful complexity stabilizes; controls bloat with less quality loss than a fixed limit chosen without problem-specific knowledge. | Suggests our structural-early/parametric-late schedule could additionally carry a *dynamically tightening* structural budget rather than (or alongside) a fixed cost term, tightening progressively as generations proceed. | *Genetic Programming and Evolvable Machines* 10(2), 2009; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10710-008-9075-9 | [snippet] |

**Neutral drift of size under mutation-only search, synthesized:** the size evolution equation's clean prediction — no selection ⇒ zero expected drift, only variance (a random walk bounded by operator support) — means any *directional* neutral drift observed empirically is diagnostic evidence of an asymmetric operator (or an unbalanced grammar prior, §2), not evidence of an inherent "entropy pulls toward growth" force. This is also the generic behavior of any mutation-only structured search: absent selection, the population's structure distribution converges to the stationary distribution of the (grammar, operator) Markov chain, i.e., its implicit prior — which is exactly why our own neutral-drift experiment should be read as a *diagnostic of operator/grammar asymmetry*, not a surprising emergent phenomenon. Concretely, if "add digit" and "remove digit" are not applied with matched probability and matched expected magnitude (e.g., remove is only legal on leaves, or insertion injects more substructure per "add" than a "remove" deletes), the walk has genuine drift even at zero selection pressure — matching our reported result exactly.

## 4. Complexification and innovation protection

| Work | Year | Key result | Relevance to our grammar | URL/DOI | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stanley & Miikkulainen, "Evolving Neural Networks through Augmenting Topologies" (**NEAT**) | 2002 | Four coupled mechanisms: (1) **minimal starting topology** — populations start with no hidden structure and grow only via structural mutation, so complexity is earned, not assumed; (2) **speciation** — a compatibility-distance metric (weighted excess genes, disjoint genes, average weight difference, computed via historical markings) groups genomes into species; reproduction/competition happens primarily *within* species; (3) **explicit fitness sharing**, f'ᵢ = fᵢ / Σⱼ sh(dᵢⱼ), derating fitness within crowded species so no one species can dominate the population purely by size; (4) **historical markings** (innovation numbers assigned at the moment a structural mutation is created) let crossover align genes by ancestry rather than position, avoiding "competing conventions" damage. Stanley's stated justification: a new structural innovation needs protected time for its new parameters to be optimized before it must compete on raw fitness against already-optimized topologies. | The single most directly transferable "protect innovation until it can be fairly evaluated" mechanism: a new digit/phalanx/palm-joint is exactly the kind of genuinely-new-but-currently-under-optimized change NEAT's speciation is built to protect. But full speciation assumes time for within-species co-adaptation before re-evaluation, which a short generation budget with one shared, non-resettable controller does not straightforwardly provide — flagged as a mismatch to resolve in the synthesis, not a direct transplant. | *Evolutionary Computation* 10(2):99-127; https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~ai-lab/pubs/stanley.ec02.pdf | [verified] |
| Hornby, "ALPS: The Age-Layered Population Structure for Reducing the Problem of Premature Convergence" | 2006 (GECCO) | Population is split into **age layers**; an individual's age is 1 + the age of its oldest parent (tracking how long its lineage's building blocks have existed, not calendar time); individuals compete/breed only within their own layer or after aging into the next one, and the youngest layer is periodically refilled with entirely new random individuals, guaranteeing a continuous supply of fresh material that never has to compete against fully-optimized old lineages. Reported to significantly reduce premature convergence and roughly double solution quality on the paper's antenna-design benchmark versus a plain EA. | An age-based (not topology-based) alternative to NEAT speciation for protecting new structural material — arguably a better match for a problem where "protection" is really about giving a newly grown digit/phalanx time to be re-evaluated fairly against noise and an under-adapted controller, not about protecting distinct topological species. Requires no genetic-distance metric, unlike NEAT. | GECCO 2006; https://www.cs.york.ac.uk/rts/docs/GECCO_2006/docs/p815.pdf | [verified] |
| Lehman & Stanley, "Abandoning Objectives: Evolution Through the Search for Novelty Alone" | 2011 (*Evolutionary Computation* 19(2):189-223) | Replaces the fitness objective with a novelty score = mean distance, in a domain-specific behavior space, to the k-nearest neighbors among the current population *and* an accumulating archive of past novel individuals. Central empirical claim: on deceptive domains (e.g., maze navigation with dead-end traps), objective-based search gets stuck in local optima that look locally best but are behaviorally similar to each other, while novelty search — rewarding any previously unseen behavior regardless of task performance — reliably discovers the necessary stepping-stone behaviors and reaches the goal more often. | Structurally relevant as a diagnostic/mitigation for our controller-bias pathology: a design that needs a controller adaptation the controller hasn't made yet looks like a fitness regression *right now* even if it is a valuable stepping stone — a textbook deceptive-fitness setup. A lightweight structural/behavioral novelty bonus is a candidate counterweight to pure fitness-based truncation. | https://www.cs.swarthmore.edu/~meeden/DevelopmentalRobotics/lehman_ecj11.pdf | [verified] |
| Mouret & Clune, "Illuminating Search Spaces by Mapping Elites" (**MAP-Elites**) | 2015 | Discretizes a chosen low-dimensional feature/behavior space into a grid; maintains exactly one elite (best-fitness-so-far) per cell, replacing it only when a new candidate lands in that cell with higher fitness. Frames this explicitly as **"illumination"** — mapping the best achievable fitness at every point of a feature space — as a strict generalization of single-point "optimization" (any illumination run can be read as an optimizer by taking the best cell). | A concrete recipe for keeping structurally diverse designs (e.g., binned by digit count × palm-joint count × motor count) alive as parallel elites, rather than letting 50%-truncation collapse the population onto one structural family — converts a qualitative diversity worry into an explicit, implementable archive design. | arXiv:1504.04909; https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04909 | [verified] |
| Fontaine, Togelius, Nikolaidis, Hoover, "Covariance Matrix Adaptation for the Rapid Illumination of Behavior Space" (**CMA-ME**) | 2020 (GECCO) | Combines a MAP-Elites archive with CMA-ES-style adaptive Gaussian "emitters" that locally hill-climb toward filling/improving archive cells, using archive-improvement (new cell filled, or existing elite beaten) as the adaptation signal rather than a single scalar objective; reported to more than double MAP-Elites' quality-diversity score on the benchmarked domains. | Shows step-size adaptation (§6) and archive-based structural diversity (§4/§9) are not mutually exclusive — relevant if a MAP-Elites-style archive is adopted and its within-cell parametric refinement (our small-step operators) is given a CMA-style adaptive step. | arXiv:1912.02400; https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02400 | [verified] |
| Brant & Stanley, Minimal Criterion (Novelty) Search / Minimal Criterion Coevolution | 2015/2017 | Individuals need only clear a low, binary **viability threshold** (e.g., "solves at least one paired problem") to be eligible to reproduce; no explicit optimization or novelty/behavior-archive pressure is used beyond that gate, and within the viable set selection is close to unranked/drift — shown, applied to two coevolving populations, to produce broad diversity of functioning solutions in a single run with minimal machinery. | The cheapest possible innovation-protection mechanism: gate reproduction on a coarse pass/fail viability criterion (e.g., "does this design's controller clear a minimum competence bar") instead of ranking a noisy continuous fitness, and let within-threshold structural diversity survive unranked — worth considering as a supplement to truncation specifically for designs whose viability is reliable even when their precise rank is not. | GECCO 2015/2017; http://www.cmap.polytechnique.fr/~nikolaus.hansen/proceedings/2017/GECCO/proceedings/proceedings_files/pap140s3-file1.pdf | [snippet] |

**Synthesis — three axes for "protecting innovation before its value is realized":** NEAT groups by *topological similarity* (protects a class of related mutations from competing against unrelated, more-mature designs and needs a compatibility-distance metric); ALPS groups by *lineage age* (protects anything recently structurally changed, regardless of what it looks like, and needs only a generation counter); novelty search / QD groups by *behavioral or structural niche* (protects diversity along experimenter-chosen dimensions and needs a feature space). For a short generation budget with essentially no time for NEAT-style within-species co-adaptation before re-evaluation, age-layering or a lightweight structural archive are more budget-compatible than full NEAT speciation (elaborated in the synthesis section below, tagged by whether it is a grammar or process lever).

## 5. Neutrality and evolvability

| Work | Year | Key result | Relevance to our grammar | URL/DOI | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Huynen, Stadler, Fontana, "Smoothness within Ruggedness: The Role of Neutrality in Adaptation" | 1996 (PNAS 93(1):397-401) | On RNA sequence→secondary-structure landscapes, sequences of identical structure form large connected **neutral networks** spanning genotype space; a population diffusing along such a network via neutral mutation accesses, at its network's "border," many distinct nearby structures/fitness peaks that direct uphill mutation from the starting point could not reach — neutrality is not wasted search but actively broadens subsequently reachable phenotype diversity. | Justifies treating structurally-different, fitness-tied designs as valuable coverage rather than pure noise — but also implies neutral drift is directional in whatever way the local network connectivity biases it, consistent with our own finding that neutral drift is *not* undirected (it relaxes toward the operator's growth prior, i.e., our "neutral network" is not uniformly connected across size). | https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.93.1.397 | [snippet] |
| Galván-López, Poli, Kattan, O'Neill, Brabazon, neutrality-in-GP survey | ~2008-2011 | Neutral drift's effect on search is domain- and operator-dependent, not uniformly beneficial: it can help by letting a population "tunnel" across otherwise-hard fitness plateaus into regions with better subsequent gradients, but excessive neutrality can also waste evaluation budget with no directional progress; whether it helps depends on the local structure of the specific (representation, operator)-induced neutral network. | Cautions against assuming our observed drift toward the growth prior is either obviously good (broadens coverage, à la Huynen) or obviously bad (wastes noisy, expensive controller-training evaluations) without a direct ablation (neutral moves allowed vs. suppressed) measuring realized final fitness. | *Evolving Systems* 2(3):145-163, 2011; not independently re-opened this session. | [snippet] |
| Wagner & Altenberg, "Perspective: Complex Adaptations and the Evolution of Evolvability" | 1996 | Defines evolvability as a *variational* property of the genotype-phenotype map — how the map's structure determines the distribution of phenotypic effects produced by mutation — and argues **modularity** (low pleiotropy: a mutation affects a restricted subset of traits rather than all of them) is what lets a system be adaptively fine-tuned without cross-trait fitness conflicts; systems under long-run selection for adaptability are argued to be pushed toward modular, hierarchical genotype-phenotype maps *as a consequence* of the evolvability requirement. | Direct argument for a grammar organized around modular, independently-mutable production units (a "digit" subtree, a "phalanx" subtree) over a monolithic parameter vector — this paper is the classical justification for why that structural choice should matter for evolvability specifically, not just interpretability. | *Evolution* 50(3):967-976; open copy https://www.sccs.swarthmore.edu/users/08/bblonder/phys120/docs/wagner.pdf | [verified] |
| Kirschner & Gerhart, "Evolvability" | 1998 (PNAS 95(15):8420-8427) | Introduces **facilitated variation**: biological evolvability comes from weakly-linked, modular, redundant, "exploratory" regulatory components sitting atop a small set of conserved, reused functional "core" processes, so regulatory mutations can recombine/redeploy the core into new phenotypes without re-inventing core machinery each time. | Analogy for separating our grammar's "conserved core" (a validated phalanx/joint primitive that is always kinematically well-formed) from a "regulatory" layer (how many primitives, in what topology) that structural operators actually mutate — supports keeping structural operators acting on composition/arrangement of pre-validated primitives, not on primitive internals. | https://science.umd.edu/classroom/bsci338-biol708/Kirshner-Gerhart-Evolvability-PNAS1998.pdf | [snippet] |
| Wagner, robustness–evolvability resolution (*Robustness and Evolvability in Living Systems*, 2005; Ciliberti, Martin & Wagner on neutral network connectivity) | 2005 and later | Resolves the apparent robustness–evolvability conflict by distinguishing genotype-level robustness (a highly robust genotype produces less phenotypic variation per mutation, trading off against evolvability for that individual) from population-level phenotypic robustness: when many distinct, robust genotypes map to one phenotype (a broad neutral network), the population collectively has access, via neutral drift across that network, to a much larger set of one-mutation-away alternative phenotypes than any single robust genotype suggests. | Robustness and evolvability are not simply opposed for our grammar: a design that is kinematically robust to small mutations (few broken/degenerate results) is not automatically less evolvable, provided the *population* maintains diversity of distinct robust genotypes for similar phenotypes — motivates diversity maintenance (§9) as a precondition for evolvability, not only a hedge against premature convergence. | Princeton Univ. Press, 2005 (book); resolving article https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rspb/article/275/1630/91/76655 | [snippet] |

## Synthesis: from theory to our grammar (≤100 lines)

**Framing.** The balance that matters here is **expressiveness vs. tractability**: a grammar that can express enough diverse, useful hand designs while staying simple enough that operators remain local, reversible, and cheap to reason about. Every recommendation below is tagged **[GRAMMAR]** (owned by the production set and operator definitions — expressivity, simplicity, operator locality/reversibility, size-neutrality of mutation) or **[PROCESS]** (owned by the evolutionary run — selection, diversity preservation, innovation protection, noise handling). This split matters because a grammar can be perfectly balanced in isolation and still collapse under a process that has no diversity floor, and conversely no process-level fix repairs a grammar whose operators are fundamentally non-local or asymmetric. Guidance below is stated as a function of budget shape (generations G, population/eval budget N, survival fraction s, offspring-per-survivor k) rather than anchored to any one run size, since the number of stages and their lengths may change.

**1. Defining and testing "balance." [GRAMMAR, measured under PROCESS-free conditions]** Balance is a measurable, per-operator property, not a qualitative description, with two components that must be tested separately and *without selection* (mutation-only runs): (a) **grammar prior size-neutrality** — uniform random derivation from the grammar alone should not have a strong inherent bias toward very large/small trees (arXiv:2204.07410, §2); (b) **operator symmetry** — paired structural moves (add/remove digit, insert/delete phalanx) should induce matched expected size-change magnitude and matched application probability, exactly as RJMCMC (§6) requires exact-inverse proposal pairs and as the size evolution equation (§3) requires for zero-drift neutral dynamics. Operationally: run mutation-only, no-selection drift and measure the first moment of Δ(size) per operator class; balance means this is ≈0 for the paired structural set (or a deliberately non-zero target if a growth prior is explicitly wanted, e.g. our insertion prior). Our own finding — "neutral drift relaxes toward the growth prior" — is direct diagnostic evidence of exactly this kind of asymmetry, not an inherent property of grammars in general, and it is entirely a **[GRAMMAR]** issue: it appears with zero selection, so no process-level fix addresses it.

**2. A minimal-start complexification scheme, as a function of budget. [GRAMMAR defines the seed and structural operators; PROCESS defines the protection schedule]** NEAT's "start minimal, complexify via structural mutation, protect immature complexity via within-species relative fitness" (§4) is the template, but full speciation assumes co-adaptation time between structural mutation and re-evaluation that a short, single-shared-controller budget may not provide. A budget-general version: **[GRAMMAR]** seed generation 0 near-minimal (few digits/phalanges, few motors) so complexity is earned rather than assumed, matching Wagner & Altenberg's modularity argument (§5) for why a hierarchical grammar should be exploited from a small seed rather than a large one. **[PROCESS]** when a structural mutation creates new material, exempt that lineage from full rank competition for a grace period expressed as a *fraction* of the budget (e.g., ~5-10% of total generations, or 2-3 generations per "structural stage" if the run is staged) rather than a fixed generation count — ALPS's age-layer logic (§4), not NEAT's topological-species logic, since it needs no compatibility-distance metric and reuses a generation counter we already have. The grace period should scale with however many generations the controller needs, empirically, to have seen enough of the new structural class to score it fairly (measurable, §7 below).

**3. Which parsimony mechanism suits noisy shared-controller fitness. [PROCESS]** Parsimony/cost is a selection-time mechanism, not a grammar property — the grammar's job is to make growth-vs-shrink symmetric by default (item 1); parsimony pressure is how the *process* decides whether to prefer smaller designs beyond that symmetric default. Covariant parsimony pressure (§3) needs a reliable per-generation size-fitness covariance estimate, which is exactly the statistic noisy fitness corrupts most. **Lexicographic parsimony pressure** (Luke & Panait, §3) fits better: it only invokes a size preference as a tie-break among fitness-statistically-indistinguishable individuals — precisely when a size preference should dominate anyway, since fitness cannot be reliably ranked there regardless. A **Tarpeian pre-evaluation gate** (§3) is a good complement because it acts *before* the expensive, noisy controller-training step, bounding structural growth without touching the noisy signal at all, with a tunable, fitness-independent dial (p). Our existing weighted cost term is a third, coarser lever in the same family — the three are not mutually exclusive.

**4. Diversity/innovation-protection mechanism, sized to budget. [PROCESS]** With truncation-and-no-recombination (one offspring per survivor), diversity loss per generation is worse than the raw survival fraction s alone would suggest, because there is no crossover to re-create combinations lost by truncation (§9). In order of implementation cost, scaled to any (G, N, s, k): (i) **random immigrants** — reserve a small fixed fraction of each generation's evaluation budget (order 5-10%, independent of G) for fresh grammar-prior samples, unconditionally; (ii) **deterministic-crowding-style replacement** — since there is already one offspring per survivor, have each offspring compete against its own parent rather than being globally ranked, a near-zero-cost change; (iii) if a coarse structural feature vector (digit count × palm-joint count × motor count) is cheap to compute, a **MAP-Elites-style archive** (§4) run alongside the main truncation population is the most theoretically well-matched mechanism, making diversity maintenance explicit along dimensions we actually care about. Full NEAT speciation and full island models are judged too parameter-heavy for a short budget (§4, §9) unless G is large enough to afford within-species or within-island co-adaptation time.

**5. Adapting operator mixtures online. [borderline — operators are GRAMMAR, the mixture schedule is PROCESS]** Bandit-based adaptive operator selection with extreme-value/AUC credit assignment (Fialho et al., §6) is the best-matched framework: it is explicitly designed for noisy, non-stationary reward, and our mixture ratio legitimately should shift from structural-heavy to parametric-heavy as a fraction of the budget elapses, not at a fixed generation number. Running bandit-AOS as an ablation against a fixed structural-early/parametric-late schedule both validates the schedule (does the bandit converge to something resembling it, at whatever G is used) and potentially discovers a better schedule automatically — worth doing because §6 found essentially no direct theoretical precedent for hand-fixed structural-vs-parametric annealing, so any fixed schedule is an empirical choice that should be checked against an adaptive baseline, not assumed near-optimal.

**6. Handling noise in selection. [PROCESS]** Three levers, in order of implementation cost, all independent of G: (i) **re-evaluate elites** each generation rather than trusting a stored historical score (§7) — cheap, directly prevents "false elite" entrenchment; (ii) **soften the truncation cutoff** near the survival-fraction boundary (stochastic/tournament-style survival for individuals within noise-width of the cutoff), since that is provably where a hard truncation cut is most exposed to misranking (§7); (iii) if partial/cheap sub-evaluations of controller training are available, a **Hoeffding-race-style adaptive resampling** scheme (§7) reallocates evaluation budget toward genuinely close calls, the most implementation-heavy of the three but the most principled.

**7. What to measure to know grammar-plus-process is balanced — the balance test suite.** Each item below states the metric, a concrete pass criterion, and which layer it audits.

**8. Fairness under the shared controller — explicitly out of the grammar's scope.** Section 8's literature (Hinton & Nowlan's Baldwin effect, DERL's morphological Baldwin effect, the control-fitness-confound paper, Cheney et al.'s bootstrapping problem) all describe a controller/process-level dynamic: fitness is biased toward designs the current shared controller already handles well, and disentangling "genuinely learnable" (Baldwin-desirable) from "merely already-familiar" (confound-undesirable) requires an ablation on the *controller/evaluation* side (e.g., extra fine-tuning budget for newly-mutated designs before scoring), not a grammar change. This should be tracked as a controller-design and evaluation-protocol question; the grammar's only obligation here is to keep structural mutations *local enough* (item 1-2 above) that the controller has a reasonable chance of generalizing to a new design from nearby ones it already handles — which is an argument for grammar locality, not for grammar bias-correction.

### What our experiments did that the literature would criticize
- **No noise-aware selection mechanism at the truncation cutoff [PROCESS].** §7's literature is consistent that a hard truncation cutoff under noisy fitness concentrates misranking damage exactly at the boundary; nothing in our reported results describes re-evaluation, resampling, or cutoff-softening, which the noisy-ES and racing literature would flag as leaving selection accuracy on the table for free.
- **No recombination to offset truncation's diversity loss, and no explicit diversity-preservation mechanism reported [PROCESS]** (random immigrants, crowding, niching, or an archive) — §9's literature predicts truncation-plus-no-crossover loses diversity faster than the raw survival fraction suggests, over many generations.
- **The structural-early/small-step-late schedule was hand-fixed, not derived from an online-adaptive mechanism [borderline GRAMMAR-operator/PROCESS-schedule].** §6's bandit-AOS literature suggests at minimum an ablation against an adaptive baseline, especially since this exact scheduling problem has thin direct theoretical grounding.
- **The controller-bias pathology was observed but not yet disentangled into Baldwin-effect-desirable vs. control-fitness-confound-undesirable components [PROCESS/controller].** The closest published analogues (DERL, the 2025 brain-body co-optimization pathology paper, Cheney et al.) all treat this distinction as requiring an explicit ablation, not something safe to leave unexamined — and it is not a grammar fix.
- **The negligible-duplicates finding (E4) was not stress-tested at a larger population/design-space scale [GRAMMAR].** Fine at the current scale per §5, but the canonical-hashing literature pattern suggests re-checking if either grows substantially.
- **No operator-symmetry (exact-inverse) audit was reported for structural add/remove pairs [GRAMMAR].** §3/§6 both point to this as the most direct, cheapest diagnostic for *why* neutral drift is directional — it is a gap in method, not just in mitigation.

### 5 sources to read in full first
1. **Poli & McPhee, size evolution equation / "Parsimony Pressure Made Easy"** — https://cswww.essex.ac.uk/staff/rpoli/gp-field-guide/113Bloat.html — the exact theoretical decomposition of size drift under selection vs. neutral dynamics; directly explains and lets you formally test "neutral drift relaxes toward the growth prior" as a [GRAMMAR] operator-symmetry question.
2. **Hornby, ALPS** — https://www.cs.york.ac.uk/rts/docs/GECCO_2006/docs/p815.pdf — the most budget-compatible [PROCESS] innovation-protection mechanism; read for the exact age-computation and layer-promotion rules before adapting the grace-period scheme.
3. **Fialho, Da Costa, Schoenauer, Sebag, bandit-based adaptive operator selection** — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10472-010-9213-y — the operator-mixture-scheduling framework best matched to noisy, non-stationary reward; read before deciding whether to keep a fixed structural-early/small-step-late schedule.
4. **"Evolutionary Brain-Body Co-Optimization Consistently Fails to Select for Morphological Potential"** — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.17464 — closest published diagnosis of our exact controller-bias pathology, with a proposed disentangling ablation; this is controller/process reading, explicitly not a grammar fix.
5. **Wagner & Altenberg, "Complex Adaptations and the Evolution of Evolvability"** — https://www.sccs.swarthmore.edu/users/08/bblonder/phys120/docs/wagner.pdf — the foundational argument for why a modular, hierarchical grammar (vs. a flat parameter vector) should matter for evolvability, not just interpretability; frames the project's basic [GRAMMAR] representational bet.
