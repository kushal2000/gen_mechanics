# Lit review: what properties of a training-morphology distribution drive controller generalization

Scope: one question — what makes a single shared, morphology-conditioned controller generalize to
unseen morphologies, including real hardware — and what that implies for a grammar whose evolved
population doubles as the controller's training distribution. ~35 min, WebSearch (exhausted after
4 queries — see note) + WebFetch on arXiv/PubMed primary sources, no downloads.

**Note on method**: the session's WebSearch budget was exhausted almost immediately (shared across
the environment, not just this task), after 4 queries returned nothing. All findings below come from
targeted WebFetch against the arXiv API (`export.arxiv.org/api/query`), arXiv abstract/HTML pages,
and PubMed — i.e. I could only chase papers I could name or guess the arXiv ID for, not do open-ended
discovery. Several named targets (Xiong et al., a morphology-agnostic RL survey, CrossDex) were
searched for on arXiv and returned zero results — either mis-titled/mis-attributed in the brief, not
on arXiv, or not yet indexed. Marked "not found" below rather than guessed.

## Synthesis

**(a) Distribution properties that drive generalization, ranked by strength of evidence**

1. **Diversity/breadth beats near-neighbor similarity, and this is now measured directly, twice.** URMA: training on *all* quadrupeds except the target beats training on the two most similar robots. DexFormer: 25→50→100 hand variants gives a monotonic accuracy gain (79.3%→84.1%). This is the single best-supported claim in the set — two independent domains (legged locomotion, dexterous hands), two independent groups, same direction.
2. **Distance from the training support predicts failure, sharply.** AnyBody's cosine-similarity analysis and its near-0% extrapolation success rate, GenLoco's "degrades gracefully to ~2x training range then fails," and LocoFormer's explicit "not exhaustive... real robots have features [we didn't cover]" all point the same way: generalization is graded and collapses once the target morphology is far (structurally or parametrically) outside what was trained on. Interpolation within a covered family is close to solved (AnyBody, MetaMorph-dynamics, GenLoco); cross-family extrapolation is the open, largely unsolved problem everywhere it was tested.
3. **Structural variety matters as its own axis, separate from parametric range.** MetaMorph generalizes to unseen *dynamics* well but drops on unseen *kinematics* (joint angles). GET-Zero needed an embodiment-graph bias specifically to transfer across joint-configuration/link-length *structural* changes. DexFormer only varied continuous params while holding kinematic-graph topology fixed and didn't test topology change. GraspGraphNet's structural (finger-removal) variants lose ~11 points versus in-distribution. Net: parametric range alone under-covers what "unseen morphology" means in practice; branching/coupling/topology variety is a distinct, harder-to-generalize-over axis.
4. **Evolved-and-curated populations work, but no paper isolates evolution's curriculum contribution from population size or diversity.** DERL shows evolution selects for morphologies that learn faster (a real curriculum effect on the *controller's* learning), and MetaMorph explicitly guards against "rich-gets-richer" population collapse during joint training — implying that without such a guard, an evolving/selected population's curriculum value can erode. But no controlled A/B (evolved population vs. sampled population, same size/diversity, same downstream eval) was found. This is the weakest-evidenced strong claim in our brief and should be labeled as inference, not a result.
5. **Realism of geometry/dynamics is necessary but its marginal value beyond "not obviously wrong" is under-tested.** Peng et al. and Akkaya et al. both show dynamics randomization *replacing* real dynamics matching works for sim-to-real, i.e. you don't need your training distribution to be dynamically accurate — you need it to be *wide enough to contain* the real dynamics, even via brute randomization. No paper in this set tested "narrow-but-accurate" vs "wide-but-crude" geometry head-to-head for morphology (as opposed to dynamics) generalization; House of Dextra fabricates and tests real hardware but doesn't isolate geometric realism from finger-count/topology variety as the driver of its results.
6. **Anthropomorphic (human-like) bias is not obviously advantageous and was the single worst-performing family where directly tested.** House of Dextra: 3-finger radial hand beat the anthropomorphic baseline by >2.5x on real objects (15/17 vs 3/17). This cuts against an implicit assumption that "human-like" is the safe target shape; it's one weak data point but a striking one, and directly contradicts UniMorphGrasp's design choice to canonicalize everything into a human-hand-like pose space (unverified beyond abstract, so treat that tension as open, not resolved).

**(b) Implications for our grammar**

- **Widen the sampling support toward real hands' continuous limits, link lengths, and mid-chain frame rotations/offsets** — this is directly supported by (2) and by LocoFormer's explicit admission that its procedural family excluded real robots' anhedral angles/offset joint axes and that this was a live source of residual sim-to-real error even after 100k training morphologies and long-context adaptation. GenLoco's "fails past ~2x training range" and AnyBody's "extrapolation ≈ 0% success" both say: if a real hand's geometry sits outside the grammar's support, no amount of policy capacity fixes it zero-shot. Given our joint limits/link lengths/axes currently sit off-grid from Allegro/LEAP/SHARPA/Inspire/Ability, this is very likely costing real-hand transfer today, independent of anything the controller does.
- **Structural variety (digit count, coupling topology, branching) should be prioritized at least as highly as widening continuous ranges**, per (3). Adding continuous slack to the current grid (finer joint-limit/link-length resolution) addresses the parametric axis; it does not address the topology axis that MetaMorph, GET-Zero, and GraspGraphNet each show behaves differently (and can fail differently) from parametric variation. Real hands differ from our straight-rod digits structurally (mid-chain bends), not just parametrically — that's a topology/geometry-primitive gap, not a range gap, and the evidence says it needs its own fix (e.g. a grammar primitive for segment offset/rotation), not just wider sampling of existing primitives.
- **Evolve the population, but explicitly counter its tendency to narrow.** DERL's Baldwin-effect finding supports evolving (not purely sampling) the population as beneficial for controller learning speed; MetaMorph's anti-"rich-gets-richer" replay balancing shows that a population under joint selection pressure needs deliberate diversity-preservation or its curriculum value collapses. Given our project already evolves the population, the actionable implication is: instrument and defend population diversity/coverage explicitly (e.g. novelty pressure, diversity-aware selection, or periodic re-injection of wide/random morphologies) rather than assuming evolution alone will maintain broad support — nothing in this review proves evolved-vs-sampled is better for generalization, but everything found about population collapse says an *unmanaged* evolved population is a real risk.
- **More distinct morphologies, not just more samples, is a real lever** (evidence point 1). If constrained on compute, the DexFormer and URMA results argue for spending budget on breadth of distinct designs over depth of samples per design — consistent with a grammar-driven population as long as the grammar keeps producing genuinely distinct kinematic trees rather than converging.
- **Don't over-invest in matching real dynamics/geometry precisely; invest in making the support wide enough to contain it.** (5) — brute-force-widened randomization (ADR, dynamics randomization) substituted for realism in two well-verified papers. This argues our simplified/gridded geometry is less of a liability than the *coverage gap* itself — i.e., the fix is width and structural coverage, not necessarily higher-fidelity contact/actuation models.

**(c) What correlated with robustness to shifts**

- Physical stability and energy efficiency correlated with faster learning and (by DERL's account) better downstream control — a property of the morphology itself, evolvable and measurable, not just a training-distribution property.
- Non-anthropomorphic (radial, 3-finger) hand morphology outperformed anthropomorphic morphology on real-object grasping robustness in the one paper that tested this directly (House of Dextra) — tentative but notable given our project's implicit orientation toward hand-like designs.
- Structural distinctiveness/topology diversity within the training population (not merely parametric spread) correlated with which axis of unseen-morphology transfer succeeded (dynamics) vs. struggled (kinematics/topology) across MetaMorph, GET-Zero, and GraspGraphNet.
- Tolerance to variation in the agent's own actions was far higher than tolerance to variation in initial state/environment (Carvalho & Nolfi) — a caution that where in the pipeline variation is injected (controller inputs vs. embodiment initial conditions) changes how much robustness it buys.

**(d) Gaps**

- No controlled comparison of evolved vs. sampled training populations, holding size/diversity fixed, for downstream controller generalization — the single most relevant-to-us missing result.
- No paper isolates geometric/dynamic *realism* from *coverage width* for morphology (not just dynamics) generalization.
- Lipson & Pollack and a dedicated Cheney-style "reality gap for evolved robots" paper were not opened this session (PNAS/Nature not accessible via WebFetch, and search tooling was constrained) — this is a real hole in item 5 of the brief and should be revisited with a session that has search budget or direct journal access.
- CrossDex, Xiong et al., and a "morphology-agnostic RL survey 2024–2026" could not be located on arXiv under the names given — either mis-cited or non-arXiv; worth asking whoever compiled the brief for corrected pointers.
- No source directly measures "density near the target" (how many training samples are near the real target hand in morphology space) as distinct from raw coverage/inclusion — AnyBody's cosine-similarity analysis is the closest proxy found, and even that is correlational, not a controlled density sweep.
